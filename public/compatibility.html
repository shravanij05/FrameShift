The phones FrameShift is made for.

FrameShift exists because videos from newer iPhones often won't play on older and mid-range Android phones. The .mp4 it produces is built for the phones below.

Why iPhone videos fail on these phones

  • HEVC (H.265) video. iPhones save video in this newer compression by default. Many older and mid-range Android phones can't play it smoothly, or at all.
  • HDR and Dolby Vision. Recent iPhones record 10-bit HDR. On phones without HDR support, colours turn grey, green or pink.
  • 4K and 60 frames per second. This is more than these phones can decode without stuttering.
  • The .mov wrapper. Some apps refuse .mov files even when the video inside is fine.

What FrameShift changes

File type.mp4, with the index at the start so playback begins straight away
VideoH.264, High profile, Level 4.1
ColourStandard 8-bit. HDR clips are converted to normal colours.
SizeUp to 1920 pixels on the longer side (1080p). 4K is scaled down.
Frame rateCapped at 30 frames per second
AudioAAC stereo, 44.1 kHz, 128 kbps

Not covered

  • Phones not listed above. The output is standard H.264, so many other phones will play it, but these are the ones it's made for.
  • Damaged files, or files with no video track.
  • Files larger than the upload limit on the Convert page.

Converting always costs a little quality, and 4K becomes 1080p. That's on purpose: these phones can't decode more.
